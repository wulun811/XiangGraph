The problem beneath parallelism

Locks protect writes.
They do not protect meaning.

A file can remain untouched while the assumptions behind an agent's work become obsolete. A contract changes. A policy revision invalidates a generated snapshot. A new relationship changes what must be checked. This is semantic staleness — often discovered only after more work has been built on top of it.

Typical coordination baseline

Coordinate activity

Task graphs, messages, and file locks are useful — but semantic freshness is often left to agents, tests, or a human to infer.

  • ×A write lock cannot prevent cross-file business drift.
  • ×A message may announce a change without defining what must be invalidated or re-proven.
  • ×A global restart can discard independent progress.
XiangGraph's design direction

Coordinate validity

Keep agents autonomous while making the consequences of change explicit, scoped, and reviewable.

  • ✓Track which work depends on a changed relationship or contract.
  • ✓Turn change into targeted refresh obligations and evidence re-binding.
  • ✓Hold affected work while unrelated work remains eligible to continue.

Conceptual design contrast, not a benchmark against every orchestration framework.

The coordination model

Do not stop every agent.
Stop only what became stale.

The Director composes a small set of situational protocols before execution. Agents still decide how to do their work. When a versioned change arrives, the intended response is local: identify affected scope, create the right obligation, notify the dependent agent, and require evidence against the current snapshot.

01 / COMPOSE

Plan the rules, not every move

A Director selects reusable protocols for dependencies, review, conflict, recovery, or escalation instead of hard-coding one giant workflow.

02 / LOCALIZE

Keep the blast radius small

A changed premise should invalidate only the work that depends on it. Unrelated agents should not be frozen because something changed elsewhere.

03 / PROVE

Evidence before eligibility

Receiving a message is not refreshing work. Passing a check is not authorization. Eligibility is not an external commit.

Architecture

Autonomy at the edge.
Invariants at the boundary.

XiangGraph is designed to avoid turning the coordinator into another all-knowing agent loop. Models propose and perform work; the coordination layer makes scope, versions, obligations, and decision boundaries explicit.

01

Autonomous agents

Agents choose implementation details inside their assigned Work and authority. The Director does not micromanage every tool call.

Agent discretion
02

Director-composed protocols

Reusable situational protocols describe what to do when dependencies change, evidence expires, conflicts arise, recovery is uncertain, or escalation is required.

Planning / composition
03

Semantic coordination core

Candidate contracts distinguish Work from Attempt, obligations from observations, evidence from authorization, and current eligibility from commitment.

A green check is not a production commit.

XiangGraph explores a stronger semantic contract for multi-agent work. The public repository contains runnable PHP slices, integration checks, and sanitized replays. It does not yet claim a production distributed runtime, OS-level fencing, continuously enforced future-change monitoring, real external publication, or statistically established superiority over other orchestration approaches.
